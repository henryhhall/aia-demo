Ice dams are a common and costly winter problem for homeowners in Connecticut. When snow accumulates on a roof, heat escaping from the home can melt the snow from underneath. As the melted water runs down the roof slope, it reaches the colder eaves and gutters, where it refreezes. This cycle creates a thick ridge of ice—an ice dam—that prevents water from draining properly. Instead, water backs up behind the dam, seeping under shingles and eventually leaking into walls, ceilings, and insulation.

Understanding how to prevent ice dams can save you thousands of dollars in repairs and prevent structural damage to your home.

## How to Prevent Ice Dams

Preventing ice dams requires a mix of immediate maintenance and long-term improvements to your home's thermal efficiency.

### 1. Improve Attic Insulation
The most effective way to prevent ice dams is to keep your roof's temperature consistent with the outdoor air. Upgrading your attic insulation keeps warm air inside your living spaces rather than escaping into the attic. In Connecticut, an R-value of R-49 to R-60 is recommended for attics.

### 2. Seal Warm Air Leaks
Exhaust fans, recessed lights, chimneys, and attic hatches are common culprits for transferring heat to your attic. Seal these air leaks with caulk, expanding foam, or weatherstripping to prevent warm air from rising directly to the underside of the roof deck.

### 3. Ensure Proper Ventilation
Attic ventilation draws cold outdoor air into the attic space and exhausts warmer air, keeping the roof deck cold. Check that your soffit, ridge, and gable vents are clear of insulation and debris so air can flow freely.

### 4. Clear Accumulating Snow
After heavy snowfalls, use a specialized roof rake to clear snow from the first three to six feet of your roof's edge. Never climb onto a snowy or icy roof yourself; instead, work safely from the ground.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Ice Dam Damage?

In most cases, standard CT homeowners insurance policies cover water damage resulting from an ice dam—such as ruined drywall, buckled flooring, or mold remediation. However, the cost to physically remove the ice dam itself or repair the exterior shingles is typically not covered, as this falls under home maintenance.
