Planning a wedding is one of the most exciting times in a couple's life, but it also represents a significant financial investment. From deposits for venues and caterers to custom attire and floral arrangements, the costs can add up quickly. If an unexpected emergency forces you to cancel or postpone your big day, you could be left with substantial financial losses.

This is where wedding insurance (also known as special event insurance) comes in. It protects your investment and ensures that your financial future isn't derailed by circumstances beyond your control.

## Types of Wedding Insurance Coverage

Wedding insurance typically falls into two main categories: Cancellation/Postponement coverage and Event Liability coverage.

### 1. Cancellation or Postponement Coverage
This coverage reimburses you for non-refundable deposits and expenses if you must cancel or reschedule the wedding due to reasons such as:
* **Severe Weather**: A major storm, hurricane, or blizzard that prevents the couple, key family members, or a majority of guests from reaching the venue.
* **Illness or Injury**: Sudden illness or injury to the bride, groom, or close family members.
* **Vendor Bankruptcy**: A venue, caterer, or photographer goes out of business or fails to show up, leaving you to find a last-minute replacement.

### 2. Special Event Liability Coverage
Event liability coverage protects you if you are held responsible for property damage to the venue or bodily injury to a guest during the rehearsal, wedding, or reception. Many premier CT wedding venues actually require couples to purchase this coverage and present a Certificate of Insurance (COI) before the event date.

## What is Typically Excluded?

It is important to read the fine print. Wedding insurance policies generally do not cover:
* **Change of Heart**: If one or both partners decide not to go through with the wedding, the policy will not pay out.
* **Pre-existing Medical Conditions**: Cancellations due to known health issues may be excluded.
* **Extreme Weather without Official Warnings**: Minor rain or snow that does not prevent travel is usually not covered.

## Is Wedding Insurance Worth It?

If you are hosting a small, casual backyard wedding with minimal deposits, you may not need cancellation coverage. However, if your venue requires general liability insurance, or if you have paid thousands of dollars in non-refundable venue and catering deposits, wedding insurance provides invaluable peace of mind. A basic policy can start as low as $150 to $300.
