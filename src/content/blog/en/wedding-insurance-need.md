- **Special Event Liability Insurance is a** short-term general liability contract providing $1,000,000 to $2,000,000 in coverage according to 2025 Insurance Information Institute report standards for third-party bodily injury and venue property damage during the rehearsal dinner, ceremony, and reception.
- **Host Liquor Liability Coverage is a** specialized legal liability protection that defends and indemnifies the wedding hosts if a guest who was served alcohol at the reception causes an off-site auto accident or injury.
- **Wedding Cancellation & Postponement Coverage is a** first-party financial reimbursement policy that recovers lost non-refundable deposits when severe weather, sudden illness, active military deployment, or vendor default forces rescheduling.

## What Are the Two Main Types of Wedding Insurance Coverage in Connecticut?

Wedding insurance is a dual-structure special event protection plan consisting of Third-Party Special Event Liability—which satisfies venue Certificate of Insurance mandates and covers guest injuries or alcohol incidents—and First-Party Cancellation or Postponement Coverage, which reimburses lost non-refundable vendor deposits caused by unexpected emergencies.

Understanding how both parts work ensures you meet your venue's legal contract while protecting your savings.

### How Does Cancellation or Postponement Coverage Protect Non-Refundable Deposits?

Wedding cancellation coverage is a first-party financial indemnity protection that reimburses couples for non-refundable venue, catering, floral, and photography deposits when an insured peril—such as a severe hurricane, sudden hospitalization, or vendor bankruptcy—forces the ceremony or reception to be postponed.

According to a 2025 wedding claims analysis report by Travelers Insurance, vendor issues and severe weather account for over 62% of all wedding postponement claims:

- **Severe Weather:** A coastal hurricane, tropical storm, or Litchfield County blizzard that shuts down roads, knocks out venue power, or prevents the couple, officiant, or a majority of guests from attending.
- **Sudden Illness, Injury, or Military Duty:** Unexpected hospitalization of the bride, groom, or immediate family members, or sudden revocation of military leave.
- **Vendor Bankruptcy or No-Show:** A banquet hall, caterer, florist, or wedding photographer closes down or fails to appear, reimbursing both your lost deposit and the added cost of booking a last-minute replacement.
- **Lost Attire, Rings, and Gifts:** Optional sub-limits cover damaged couture wedding gowns, lost wedding bands, ruined wedding cakes, and stolen reception gifts.

### Why Do Connecticut Wedding Venues Require Special Event and Host Liquor Liability?

Special event and host liquor liability is a third-party risk transfer policy required by Connecticut banquet facilities, vineyards, and municipal parks according to a 2025 venue survey report, providing $1,000,000 in primary premises liability and alcohol defense while naming the owner as Additional Insured.

According to a 2025 Connecticut Insurance Department (CID) consumer report and Connecticut General Statutes § 30-102 (Dram Shop Act), commercial alcohol purveyors and social hosts face up to $250,000+ in statutory and common-law liability per injured person if an overserved guest causes a motor vehicle collision after leaving a reception. Most premier Connecticut venues require couples to present a Certificate of Insurance (COI) 30 days before the wedding showing $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate liability with zero deductible.

## What Is Excluded Under a Standard Wedding Insurance Policy?

Wedding insurance exclusions are contractual policy boundaries that deny reimbursement for voluntary change of heart by the bride or groom, pre-existing medical conditions known before policy binding, mild rain that does not halt travel, and commercial vendor errors not resulting in bankruptcy or physical absence.

Reading the policy exclusions carefully prevents surprises during the planning process:

- **Change of Heart ("Cold Feet"):** According to 2025 Insurance Information Institute report guidelines, if either partner decides to call off the wedding, traditional cancellation policies pay $0 (though a few carriers offer a specialized "Change of Heart" rider if purchased at least 12 months in advance, covering only innocent third-party parent financiers).
- **Pre-Existing Medical Conditions:** Cancellations caused by a known illness or medical condition treated within 60 to 180 days prior to buying the policy are excluded.
- **Minor Unfavorable Weather:** A rainy afternoon or light snow that dampens outdoor photos without closing roads or shutting down the venue is not a covered cancellation trigger.
