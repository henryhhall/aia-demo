### How Does Auto Insurance Cover Deer Strikes vs. Swerving Crashes in Autumn?

Animal collision classification is an insurance claims distinction wherein striking a deer directly triggers Comprehensive (Other Than Collision) coverage with no at-fault surcharge, whereas swerving into a guardrail, stone wall, or oncoming vehicle is classified as an at-fault Collision claim that raises premiums for three to five years.

Autumn in Litchfield County brings peak white-tailed deer movement right at dusk, the exact window when teens are driving home from varsity sports practices at Watertown High or evening study sessions at Taft. Every parent in Watertown should teach their teen one rule before they drive at dusk: Never swerve into another lane or off the road to miss a deer.

- **If a driver hits a deer directly:** The damage is covered under Comprehensive (Other Than Collision) coverage. Comprehensive claims generally carry a lower deductible and do not trigger an at-fault collision surcharge on your policy.
- **If a teen swerves to miss a deer and hits a guardrail, stone wall, utility pole, or oncoming car:** Insurance carriers classify that crash as an at-fault Single-Vehicle Collision (or multi-car liability claim). You pay your Collision deductible, and an at-fault crash on a teen's record can raise household premiums for three to five years.
